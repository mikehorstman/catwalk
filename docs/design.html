Catwalk · Design

What good looks like

The cat is the whole product. Right now it is a 72-pixel paw icon. The design work is mostly about fixing that, then making every screen answer one question clearly.

What good app design is

These come from Apple’s iOS guidelines, Material’s accessibility guidance, and Duolingo’s published design write-ups. I list where each comes from. Where it is my own judgment, I say so.

1. One main thing per screen

Limit the controls on screen. Keep secondary actions available but out of the way.

Apple: “Designing for iOS” best practices.

2. Put what people touch where thumbs reach

Controls in the middle or bottom of the screen are easier to reach one-handed.

Apple: “Designing for iOS” best practices.

3. Adapt to the person

Support Dark Mode, larger text, and both orientations where it makes sense. Use standard platform controls so assistive technology works without extra effort.

Apple: same page. Material: “Designing” accessibility.

4. One clear path

Duolingo’s redesign put everything on a single path so learners never wonder what to do next.

Duolingo: “the new learning path”.

5. Celebrate the moments that matter

Duolingo redesigned its streak milestone because the old one was “cute, but not quite celebratory enough”. They also made the metaphor universal, not culture-specific, and made it shareable.

Duolingo: “how we brought the streak milestone to life”.

6. A character people care about

Characters carry emotion and cheer people on. They are not decoration. This one is my judgment, supported by Duolingo’s approach. It matters most for an app whose whole premise is a pet.

What Catwalk looks like now

I read the Home screen and the theme. Honest audit:

GapThe cat is a paw icon. Icons.pets tinted cream, charcoal, or ginger. Three coat choices on onboarding look identical apart from colour. This is the single biggest gap.

GapTwo progress bars compete. Steps and level look the same. The steps are what matters today.

GapThe step number is small. It is a titleLarge line of text. It should be the largest thing on the screen.

GapNo celebration. Completing the goal swaps a progress bar for text that says “Goal complete”. No motion, no sound, no haptic.

GapStreak is hidden. It only appears on History. It is the main habit driver.

GapGeneric title. The app bar says “Home”.

GapLight mode only.

GoodWarm palette, calm layout. The brown seed and paper background feel right. Keep them.

GoodHonest states. “Allow step access” and “not available” are clear and never fake numbers.

The plan

Ordered so each step is visible on its own and builds toward the room slice. Nothing here changes game rules.

D1. The cat becomes the hero

  • Replace the paw icon with a real cat illustration.
  • Three coats that look different, not just recoloured.
  • Three moods that look different: happy, content, sad. Plus a “gone” state for runaway.
  • Gentle idle motion: breathing, blinking, a tail flick.
  • Same cat component reused on Home, Cat, and onboarding, and later in the room.

D2. Home answers one question

How far am I today?

  • Big step number, the largest text on screen.
  • A ring around or under the cat instead of a thin bar, so the cat and the goal are one picture.
  • Level and XP shrink to a quiet line. Streak gets a visible spot.
  • Settings stays top-right. Anything you tap often sits lower.
  • Title becomes the cat’s name, not “Home”.

D3. Feedback and celebration

  • The ring animates when steps change, not just jumps.
  • Goal reached: a short animation, a haptic tap, and “+50 XP” flying to the level bar.
  • Level up: its own bigger moment.
  • Streak milestones (3, 7, 30 days): bigger still, and shareable later.
  • Respect the phone’s reduce-motion setting.

D4. Accessibility and platform fit

  • Dark Mode with the same warm palette, not an inverted one.
  • Text scales with the phone’s font size without breaking layouts.
  • Contrast checked on every text and button colour.
  • Touch targets at least 48 px. Screen-reader labels on the cat, ring, and numbers.
  • Never rely on colour alone for mood or goal state.

D5. Words and empty states

  • Short, warm, cat-voiced copy. “Mimi is waiting for her walk.”
  • Permission and “unavailable” screens explain why in one line.
  • Empty History gets a friendly picture, not just text.

D6. Fold into the room (slice 3)

Once the cat is a real character, the room is where she lives. Home can eventually become that room with the ring and number overlaid. Do not build both separately.

Decisions I need from you

  1. Art style. Flat vector illustration, soft 2D with shading, or something hand-drawn. Flat vector is the fastest to animate in Flutter and scales to every screen.
  2. Who draws it. I can build a simple vector cat in code to get started. A designer or purchased asset pack would look far better. Code-drawn is a stand-in, not the final art.
  3. Dark Mode now or later. I would do it in D4, not before.
  4. Sound. Haptics only, or short sounds for goal and level-up too.

What I would do first

D1 and D2 together. They change what you see the most, and everything else builds on a cat that looks alive. I would start with a code-drawn vector cat in three moods, so you can judge the layout before any final art exists.
